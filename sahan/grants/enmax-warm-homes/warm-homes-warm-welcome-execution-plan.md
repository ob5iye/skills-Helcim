## 0. The Why — What This Project Is and Why It Matters

*Use this section, in your own voice, in the conversation with Adam.*

**Every year, families arrive in Calgary in November with nothing that prepares them for a
Canadian winter — no experience with minus-thirty windchill, no understanding of an Alberta
utility bill, no idea what a carbon monoxide alarm sounds like, and no one to ask.**

Sahan Heritage Foundation exists for exactly these families. We are a volunteer-run,
registered Alberta society serving about 1,300 newcomers a year. And what our volunteers
keep seeing, over and over, is the same winter story: a family gets their first utility
bill and cannot tell what they're paying for. They blast the heat to 26 degrees because
nobody told them 20 is enough — or the safe one. They have never heard that they can
*choose* a retailer, or that 211 exists, or that ENMAX has payment support when things
get tight.

**Warm Homes, Warm Welcome turns that first dangerous, confusing winter into a welcome.**

Three free evening sessions — dinner shared, guides in their own language, every family
leaving with a winter efficiency kit they can use *that same night*. Then the learning
locks in through the Warm Homes Challenge: a live family-versus-family game where they
have to apply what they learned — read a mock bill, make the cost comparison, sort the
recycling, and explain the reasoning out loud. People remember what they had to stand
up and defend in front of their neighbours. That's the design.

**Why it should matter to ENMAX specifically:**
this is your Energy Efficient Futures model — community energy education — delivered to
the audience that model currently doesn't reach: brand-new Calgarians who will be ENMAX
customers within their first year. Better-informed customers make better rate choices,
heat safely, and call for payment help *before* they're in collections. The financial
case and the human case point the same direction.

**What we are asking** is small on purpose — $1,500, everything else donated — because
this is our first corporate partnership and we intend to deliver it flawlessly, measure
it honestly, and report back with photos, attendance, and before-and-after knowledge
results. If it works — and it will — it becomes a repeatable winter program, and ENMAX
was there first.

**The ask:** $1,500 for meals, translated guides, and efficiency kits · an ENMAX speaker
if one is willing · recognition on all materials and in our final report.

### Why the evening is built this way

- **Eat first, teach second** — dinner is the attendance mechanism; hungry parents learn nothing.
- **Everything hands-on** — touching the CO alarm and applying weatherstripping beats any slide.
- **Competition replaces passive recap** — families have to apply what they heard, out loud, in
  front of neighbours. That is where the learning locks in.
- **The kit leaves in their hands the same night** — "make your home warmer tonight" is the whole program promise.

### 4B. The Warm Homes Challenge — Full Competition Design

**Format.** Gameshow-style up front: **2 teams × 3 adults** (drawn from different families),
standing at a table on each side of the **host**. Audience plays along on paper cards so nobody
is just watching. ~25 minutes: 3 rounds × 5 questions + bonus round.

**Why it works.** Newcomer adults often won't raise a hand to ask "dumb" questions in a lecture —
but they'll shout an answer for their team. Competition converts embarrassment into energy, and
every answer must be *explained*, which is where the teaching actually lands. It also generates
footage and scores we can put in the ENMAX report as evidence of learning.

**Mechanics.**
- Host reads question → first team to buzz (wooden spoon on table — loud, funny, free) answers
- Correct = 10 pts; wrong = opposing team steals for 5
- Bonus round answers require an **explanation**, not just the answer
- Each team gets one "ask the room" lifeline per match
- Prizes: premium LED bulb + CO alarm combo (~$15/team) + printed "Warm Homes Champion"
  certificate. Everyone still gets the standard winter kit — no family leaves empty-handed.
  Absorbed inside the $300 kits line (~$30 total).

#### Round 1 — The Money Round (bill understanding)
Every question forces a **dollar comparison between two practices** — families must reason about
real financial differences:
- *"Family A heats to 22°C all winter; Family B heats to 20°C and 17°C overnight. Who pays less, and roughly how much?"* (≈5% saved per degree lowered)
- *"Farah's bill: $180 energy + $95 delivery/fees. Ahmed's: $140 energy + $95 delivery/fees. Ahmed used less energy — why did his total bill barely drop?"* (fixed delivery charges — the most-misunderstood line on any Alberta bill)
- *"Two identical homes, identical usage. One family on a fixed-rate plan, one on the Regulated Rate. Name one risk each family takes."*
- *"Does running your dryer at noon instead of 9 PM change your Calgary bill? Why or why not?"*
- *"Which costs a family more over one winter: heating a spare room nobody uses, or leaving a second fridge running in the garage?"*

#### Round 2 — Winter-Proofing Round
- *"Your CO alarm beeps 4 times then pauses. What do you do in the next 60 seconds?"*
- *"Which saves more over one winter: a window film kit or one LED bulb? Explain."*
- *"Your pipes freeze while you're away two weeks. Name the one habit that prevents this."*
- *"True/false: a space heater next to curtains is fine if you watch it."*
- *"Where in the home should a CO alarm go — and why not right beside the furnace?"*

#### Round 3 — Bin It Right (waste management)
**Physical prop round** — the crowd-pleaser. The host tips a "mystery bin" onto the table
(pizza box, plastic bag, foil tray, banana peel, old battery, chip bag) and teams race to sort
items into blue / black / green cart props, host judging against the **City of Calgary
What Goes Where tool**. Plus spoken questions:
- *"Why does plastic film jam the recycling facility?"*
- *"Name two habits that shrink this family's black-cart volume in half."*
*Note: waste content is new since the application — confirm with Adam whether the ENMAX speaker
is comfortable with this round, or run it as the audience warm-up round instead.*

#### Bonus Round — Plan the Month
Both teams get a scenario card: a newcomer family with $200 to spend on home efficiency.
Allocate it (thermostat habits, kit items, curtains, etc.) and present in 60 seconds. Host scores
on reasoning; room votes by applause. Doubles as the session's summary — every family watching
just heard the whole curriculum applied once more.

**Host kit:** question cards with correct answers + one-line explanations on the back, score
flip-chart, wooden-spoon buzzers ×2, mystery-bin props, cart props, prize packs, certificates.
