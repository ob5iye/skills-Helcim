## 3. The Project — "Friday Welcome Nights"

### One-sentence concept
A recurring series of monthly Friday-evening welcome gatherings at one fixed Calgary venue,
where newly arrived newcomer families are welcomed, fed, oriented, and connected — hosted by 25
trained Sahan volunteer ambassadors, with each month's guests becoming next month's regulars and
future welcomers.

### The audience model (guests → regulars → hosts)
- **Returning people (community backbone):** 25 trained ambassadors host every time; past
  newcomer families return — returning attendees are the GOAL (belonging = routine + connection)
- **New people each month:** every gathering welcomes a fresh wave of 10–15 newly arrived
  families, recruited between gatherings via mosques, settlement-agency referrals, WhatsApp
  networks, libraries
- **The arc:** newcomers arrive as guests → get welcomed and oriented → return as community
  members → eventually help welcome the next wave
- **Grant rule reminder:** only the LOCATION must repeat (same venue, ≥3 times), not the people

### Recruitment of ambassadors (25 total)
1. Core: Sahan's 15 existing volunteers
2. Graduates of Sahan's own programs (people who received help make the best helpers)
3. Mosque/community announcements (2–3 announcements fill remaining ~10 spots)
4. Partner agency referrals (if a partner letter is secured, ask them to share the posting too)

### Recruitment of newcomer guests (between each gathering)
- The 3–4 week gap between gatherings = one recruitment cycle: ambassadors identify newly
  arrived families through community and faith networks, personally invite them, and prepare
  culturally appropriate food and activities for that specific wave
- Channels: mosques/faith institutions, settlement agency referrals (CCIS, Immigrant Services
  Calgary, CIWA, CIES), 211 Alberta, LINC/ESL classes, Calgary Public Library branches, ethnic
  grocery/halal shops, WhatsApp & Facebook community groups, community events with sign-up
  sheets + follow-up calls

## 4. Program Design

### Component A — Ambassador Training (Thursdays 6–9 pm, every 2 weeks, ~12 weeks)
Six modules, designed for people new to volunteering (Sahan's team self-identified: "not all of
us understand volunteering"):

| Module | Content | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Volunteering 101 & the Ambassador Role** | What volunteering is/isn't; rights & responsibilities; the ambassador role = welcomer, guide, connector — NOT counselor/lawyer/caseworker; code of conduct, confidentiality, commitment expectations | 2 |
| **2. Understanding the Newcomer Journey** | Settlement stages; the "first 90 days"; what a newcomer who has no idea what to do actually needs; trauma-informed basics; cultural humility | 1 |
| **3. Mapping Calgary's Services** | The referral map: 211 Alberta, settlement agencies, Alberta Supports, healthcare, school registration, housing, employment services, food banks. **Golden rule: ambassadors REFER, they don't advise.** Each ambassador builds a personal referral contact card | 2 |
| **4. Communication Across Languages** | Plain-language speaking; active listening; working with family members as interpreters (and when not to); phone/video interpretation resources in Calgary | 1 |
| **5. Boundaries, Ethics & Red Flags** | Never give immigration/legal advice; never handle money; never make promises; safeguarding (mental health, family violence, crisis) and exactly how to escalate; privacy | 1 |
| **6. Hands-On Practice** | Running an orientation circle; hosting a welcome gathering; one-on-one navigation role-plays using real scenarios; the simple tracking sheet (who helped, what referral made — becomes grant reporting data) | 1 |

**Modelled on:** The Connection Intercultural Association of Medicine Hat's Intercultural
Ambassador program — a proven Alberta model (won a provincial SILP Stream 1 grant for a 160-person
version: 24 months, learning circles, Ambassador Toolkit, micro-learning platform). Their format:
3-hour interactive trainings focused on local demographics/needs, with immediate practical
application; events have sold out in 2 days. Ours is a scaled pilot: adaptation of a proven model,
localized for Calgary. Optional: contact The Connection (Exec. Director: Yusuf Mohammed) for advice
or a letter. Also see AAISA (aaisa.ca) for existing settlement-sector courses/toolkits.

### Component B — Friday Welcome Nights (the funded, visible placemaking activity)
Monthly Friday 6–9 pm gatherings at ONE fixed venue (6 gatherings):
- Cultural food, music, storytelling, kids' activities
- A "welcome corner" where new families get oriented to city services
- Delivered by trained ambassadors; staff/lead supervision
- Each gathering welcomes 10–15 newly arrived families + returning community members
- **Proposed calendar (assuming mid-Dec 2026 decision, Jan 2027 start):**

| Date | Activity |
|---|---|
| Jan 14 & 28, 2027 (Thu) | Module 1 training |
| Feb 11 & 25, 2027 (Thu) | Modules 2 + 3 |
| Mar 11 & 25, 2027 (Thu) | Modules 4 + 5 |
| Mar 6, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #1** (pilot — small invited group, ambassadors practice) |
| Apr 3, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #2** — first full public wave |
| May 1, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #3** |
| May 29, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #4** |
| Jun 26, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #5** |
| Jul 24, 2027 (Fri) | **Welcome Night #6** + graduation/celebration ceremony |

- Why monthly (not weekly): gap = recruitment cycle for the next wave; prevents volunteer
  burnout; 6 well-prepared gatherings beat 12 rushed ones; still doubles the grant's 3-event minimum
- No conflicts: Thu/Fri evenings stay clear of CIP "Rise Together" Saturdays (Apr 10–Jun 26, 2027)

### Venue
- ONE fixed location within Calgary city limits (community hall, library branch, or partner venue
  such as the Somali Canadian Society of Calgary space)
- Secure written confirmation / in-kind letter from the venue
- Note: gatherings are the placemaking activity — venue consistency is a hard grant rule

## 7. Why This Project Wins (narrative angles to use)

1. **Direct funded precedent:** Centre for Newcomers (a newcomer-serving nonprofit like Sahan) won
   $10,310 for a programming project in a past cohort.
2. **The need, in our own words:** "Our volunteers regularly encounter newcomers who arrive with
   no idea what to do or where to begin." Add Calgary newcomer statistics (census/IRCC) when drafting.
3. **Guests → regulars → hosts arc:** recurring gatherings build routine, connection, belonging
   (the grant's stated placemaking theory) while each month's new families get a real entry point.
4. **Logistics already solved:** Thursday/Friday 6–9 pm confirmed free by our 15-volunteer base —
   evening times remove barriers for working newcomers. Reviewers love solved logistics.
5. **Rhythm as design:** "Between gatherings, ambassadors spend 2–3 weeks identifying newly
   arrived families through community and faith networks, extending personal invitations, and
   preparing culturally appropriate food and activities for each incoming wave. This rhythm —
   gather, prepare, welcome — mirrors how the community itself naturally operates."
6. **Sustainability:** the ambassador network outlives the grant (quarterly meetups); curriculum
   reusable for future cohorts at near-zero cost; each month's guests become next month's hosts.
7. **Measurable outcomes:** 25 ambassadors recruited, 18–20 graduated, 6 gatherings, 60–90 new
   families welcomed, total attendance tracked, pre/post ambassador knowledge surveys.
