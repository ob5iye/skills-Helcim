# 1️⃣ PROJECT DETAILS SCREEN (NHSP-ProjectDetails-Edit)

## 🟦 FIELD: Project Summary *(500 words or less)*

```
African newcomer elders in Calgary's Northeast face a daily and growing barrier to full participation in Canadian life: digital exclusion. Government services such as Old Age Security, the Guaranteed Income Supplement, Canada Revenue Agency accounts, healthcare bookings, IRCC immigration updates, and banking are now overwhelmingly online. Elders who do not read English fluently and who have never used a computer are forced to depend on adult children and grandchildren for every transaction, which causes isolation, loss of dignity, and exposure to financial fraud. Sahan Heritage Foundation works closely with this community and has heard from elders themselves that learning to use a phone, tablet, and computer is their most urgent unmet need.

Digital Confidence for Older Newcomers is a 12-month, weekend-only, senior-led project that will serve 60 African newcomer elders (age 55+) in Calgary's Northeast across three sequential cohorts of 20. Each cohort completes a structured 12-weekend digital literacy curriculum delivered Saturdays (9 AM–5 PM, 8 hours) and Sundays (1 PM–5 PM, 4 hours), with breaks scheduled around Ramadan, Eid, and the summer season.

Four elders from Cohort 1 form the Senior Leadership Committee. They co-design the curriculum, co-facilitate sessions, and serve as paid peer-mentors across all three cohorts. Cohort 1 graduates return in Cohorts 2 and 3 as alumni mentors, building a sustainable pipeline of senior leadership. Six youth volunteers from Sahan's existing community programs serve as tech buddies, creating intentional intergenerational relationships.

Anticipated results: (1) 60 newcomer elders complete the structured curriculum covering device basics, video calls, online safety and fraud awareness, MyCRA, MyAlbertaHealth, online banking, and accessing OAS and GIS; (2) 4 elders are trained and active as paid peer-mentors, with an additional 12+ alumni serving as informal mentors by project end; (3) at least 45 of the 60 elders successfully complete an online task that matters to them (a CRA login, an OAS or GIS application, a doctor's appointment booking, a video call with family overseas); (4) all participants report reduced social isolation and increased confidence accessing services independently, measured by pre/post surveys; (5) a permanent monthly Senior Tech Circle continues meeting at Sahan beyond the funded year, led by the trained mentors and using the retained equipment.

Targeted group: 60 African newcomer elders aged 55 and older living in Northeast Calgary, with strong representation from Somali, Sudanese, Eritrean, Ethiopian, and Oromo communities. The cohort prioritizes elders who arrived in Canada within the past 10 years, women, and elders living alone — all NHSP-recognized vulnerable populations. The first 20 participants are already named and committed.

This is a new program stream for Sahan Heritage Foundation. Our existing programming focuses on cultural celebrations, family recreation, and community gatherings open to all ages. Through that work elders have repeatedly told us they need direct, dignified, language-accessible help with the technology that now controls access to their pensions, healthcare, and family. This NHSP project formalizes a seniors-specific stream of our work, creates an explicit leadership role for elders within Sahan, and establishes a peer-mentor pipeline that sustains the work beyond the funded year.
```

## 🟦 FIELD: Project Activities and Timelines

```
The project runs every weekend for 12 months: Saturday 9 AM–5 PM (8 hours) and Sunday 1 PM–5 PM (4 hours), totalling 12 hours of programming per weekend. Programming pauses during Ramadan, Eid, and the peak summer travel period, leaving 40 active weekends across the year. Three sequential cohorts of 20 elders each receive a 12-weekend structured curriculum.

WEEKLY SCHEDULE (every active weekend)
• Saturday 9:00–10:30 AM — Group instruction (structured digital literacy lesson, paid instructor)
• Saturday 10:30–11:00 AM — Tea and break (peer connection, anti-isolation)
• Saturday 11:00 AM–12:30 PM — Hands-on practice in small groups of 5, each led by a Senior Peer-Mentor
• Saturday 12:30–1:30 PM — Shared lunch (culturally appropriate hot meal, anti-isolation core activity)
• Saturday 1:30–3:30 PM — One-on-one coaching with youth tech buddies (intergenerational)
• Saturday 3:30–5:00 PM — Real-life application clinic (elders work on their own CRA, OAS, banking, or telehealth tasks with support)
• Sunday 1:00–3:00 PM — Senior Tech Circle drop-in (open to current cohort + alumni, peer-led)
• Sunday 3:00–5:00 PM — Family Digital Safety Hour (elders bring a family member; fraud-prevention practice together)

PHASE 1 — Project Setup and Senior Leadership Committee (Weekends 1–2: January 17–25, 2027)
• Onboard 4 elders from Cohort 1 as the Senior Leadership Committee (SLC).
• Co-design the 12-weekend curriculum with the SLC, the Calgary Public Library digital literacy team, and a partner settlement agency.
• Procure 18 tablets, accessibility accessories, projector, screen, Wi-Fi hotspots, and charging cart.
• Confirm venue agreement with partner mosque in Northeast Calgary.
• Recruit and orient 6 youth tech-buddy volunteers.
• Baseline survey for Cohort 1 (skills, confidence, isolation, service-access barriers).

PHASE 2 — Cohort 1 Delivery (Weekends 3–14: February 6 – April 25, 2027, with Ramadan pause)
• 12 weekends of structured programming for the 20 named Cohort 1 elders.
• Modules: Device basics → Internet basics → Communication (email, WhatsApp, video calls) → Online Safety & Fraud Awareness (4 weeks, with Calgary Police Service Elder Abuse Response Team guest sessions) → Government services (MyCRA, OAS, GIS, IRCC) → Healthcare (MyAlbertaHealth, telehealth) → Banking and financial security.
• Sunday Tech Circle launches and runs each weekend.
• Pre/post skills and confidence assessments.
• Cohort 1 graduation event.

PHASE 3 — Cohort 2 Recruitment and Delivery (Weekends 15–28: May 9 – August 29, 2027, with Eid and summer pause)
• Recruit Cohort 2 (20 elders) through Cohort 1 alumni referrals, partner referrals, and outreach in Northeast Calgary.
• 12 weekends of structured programming for Cohort 2.
• Cohort 1 alumni return as informal mentors and tech-buddy reinforcements.
• Mid-project participant focus group and curriculum refinement.

PHASE 4 — Cohort 3 Recruitment and Delivery (Weekends 29–40: September 12, 2027 – January 9, 2028)
• Recruit Cohort 3 (20 elders) — recruitment is now organic through community word-of-mouth.
• 12 weekends of structured programming for Cohort 3.
• Cohort 1 and 2 alumni serve as peer-mentors, expanding the trained-mentor base to 16+.
• Two community "Digital Inclusion Days" — open weekend events where graduates showcase their skills and invite other elders to join the ongoing Tech Circle.

PHASE 5 — Sustainability and Final Evaluation (Weekend 40+: January 10–15, 2028)
• Final outcome surveys and interviews with all 60 elders.
• Knowledge-sharing event with partner organizations (settlement agencies, library, faith partners).
• Sustainability plan for the ongoing monthly Senior Tech Circle, transitioned to alumni leadership.
• Final narrative and financial report to ESDC.
```

## 🟦 FIELD: How the project meets mandatory objective(s) and priority(ies) *(500 words or less)*

```
This project supports three NHSP mandatory objectives. Because the project includes capital assets, we have selected the required minimum of two objectives plus one additional.

(1) SUPPORTING THE SOCIAL PARTICIPATION AND INCLUSION OF SENIORS — primary objective. Sixty African newcomer elders, many of whom are currently isolated at home and dependent on family for every interaction with Canadian institutions, will spend twelve hours together every active weekend during their cohort. They share meals, learn together, and build a peer community. The Sunday Senior Tech Circle is open to all cohorts simultaneously, creating cross-cohort connections that endure after each cohort's twelve weekends conclude, and continues monthly after the funded year ends. Social participation is built into every hour of the schedule, not added on top.

(2) PROVIDING CAPITAL ASSISTANCE FOR NEW AND EXISTING COMMUNITY PROJECTS AND/OR PROGRAMS FOR SENIORS — required objective for capital requests. The project procures 18 tablets, accessibility accessories (large-key keyboards, headphones, magnifiers), a portable projector and screen for group instruction, Wi-Fi hotspots, and a charging cart. All equipment remains with Sahan Heritage Foundation as a permanent loaner library and instructional kit for the ongoing Senior Tech Circle, giving the capital investment useful life well beyond the twelve months of ESDC support.

(3) ENGAGING SENIORS IN THE COMMUNITY THROUGH THE MENTORING OF OTHERS. Four elders from Cohort 1 form the Senior Leadership Committee and serve as paid peer-mentors across all three cohorts. They co-design the curriculum, lead small-group practice every Saturday, and progressively take on co-leading instructional segments. Cohort 1 alumni return for Cohorts 2 and 3 as informal mentors and tech-buddy reinforcements; by the end of the year at least 16 elders will have served in a mentoring role. Mentoring also flows the other way: six youth volunteers from Sahan's existing programs serve as tech buddies, creating intentional intergenerational mentoring relationships.

NATIONAL PRIORITY: CELEBRATING DIVERSITY AND PROMOTING INCLUSION. The project is designed by and for racialized newcomer elders from East African communities. Instruction is delivered in plain English with community-language peer support in Somali, Arabic, Amharic, Tigrinya, and Oromo. Cultural foods, prayer time, and family structures are built into the schedule rather than treated as accommodations. Outreach happens through faith leaders, women's circles, and trusted community elders to reach seniors who would never be reached by mainstream English-language programs.

A second national priority is also addressed: PREVENTING SENIOR ABUSE. Online financial fraud — phishing, romance scams, CRA-impersonation calls, and grandparent scams — disproportionately targets newcomer elders unfamiliar with Canadian institutions. A full four-week fraud-awareness module sits at the centre of every cohort's curriculum, delivered with guest sessions from the Calgary Police Service Elder Abuse Response Team and supported by multilingual fraud-prevention resources participants take home to their families.

Vulnerable populations targeted within the 60-elder cohort: newcomer elders (NHSP priority), racialized elders, low-income elders, elders living alone, women, and elders who arrived in Canada within the past 10 years.
```

## 🟦 FIELD: Describe the need in your community and how your community supports this project

```
Calgary's Northeast is home to one of the largest concentrations of African newcomer families in Western Canada, including a long-established Somali community and growing Sudanese, Eritrean, Ethiopian, and Oromo populations. Within this community, elders aged 55 and older are the most digitally excluded group. Many arrived in Canada later in life through family-class sponsorship or refugee resettlement, and have never used a computer or smartphone. They speak Somali, Arabic, Amharic, Tigrinya, or Oromo at home and have limited or no English literacy.

The need is concrete and rising. Every essential service these elders depend on has moved online: Old Age Security and Guaranteed Income Supplement applications require a My Service Canada Account; CRA correspondence is increasingly digital-first; doctors' offices use online booking systems and patient portals; pharmacies require text-message authentication for prescription refills; banks have closed branches and pushed customers to apps; IRCC immigration status and PR card renewals run through online accounts; and family members overseas can only be reached affordably through video calls. An elder without digital skills in 2026 is an elder cut off from their pension, their doctor, their bank, and their grandchildren.

This exclusion also creates real safety risks. Newcomer elders are prime targets for online financial fraud — CRA-impersonation calls, romance scams, fake government refund emails, and grandparent scams. Several elders in our community have already lost savings to these scams.

Sahan Heritage Foundation has spent the past year building deep trust with this community through cultural celebrations (Eid, community picnics) and family programs (Free Play Calgary). At every event, elders raise the same concern: they want to learn the phone and computer themselves, not depend on their children. We have already identified twenty elders by name who have committed to participating in Cohort 1, four of whom have agreed to serve on the Senior Leadership Committee. Demand for Cohorts 2 and 3 is expected to exceed our 40 remaining spaces based on conversations at recent community events.

Community support for this project is strong and concrete. A partner mosque in Northeast Calgary has agreed to donate weekend space valued at approximately $4,000 for the year. The Calgary Police Service Elder Abuse Response Team has been approached to deliver fraud-awareness guest sessions. The Calgary Public Library digital literacy team has agreed to consult on curriculum design. Six youth volunteers from Sahan's existing youth programs have committed to serve as tech buddies. A letter of support from at least one partner organization accompanies this submission. The 20 named Cohort 1 elders are themselves the strongest expression of community support: they have already named themselves to the cohort.
```

## 🟦 FIELD: Explain how the individuals will benefit

```
Sixty African newcomer elders benefit directly. By the end of the project each elder will have completed a 12-weekend digital literacy curriculum and will own usable skills in five high-impact areas: (1) communication with family overseas through video calls; (2) accessing their My Service Canada and My CRA accounts to manage OAS, GIS, and tax matters; (3) booking and attending healthcare appointments online via MyAlbertaHealth and telehealth platforms; (4) banking online safely and recognizing financial scams; and (5) navigating IRCC online services to manage immigration and citizenship matters. At least 45 of the 60 elders will have completed at least one real-life online task that matters to them with our support before the project ends. All 60 will have a community of peers they have learned alongside, which directly addresses social isolation — one of the most documented health risks facing older newcomers.

Four elders will leave the project trained as paid peer-mentors, with an additional 12 to 16 alumni serving in informal mentoring roles by year end. They will have the equipment, curriculum, and group of fellow mentors needed to sustain a monthly Senior Tech Circle for years to come.

For young people who are themselves navigating dual cultural identities, this work strengthens cultural belonging and pride.

Approximately 80 family members of the participating elders benefit. Indirectly: adult children and grandchildren currently spend significant time managing tech tasks for their elders and are freed from this burden as their parents become independent. Directly: the Sunday Family Digital Safety Hour invites one family member of each elder to attend fraud-prevention sessions together, spreading online-safety awareness across the whole household and protecting families from scams that target newcomers.

Beyond the funded year, the sustained monthly Senior Tech Circle and the retained equipment will continue benefiting additional elders in Northeast Calgary's African newcomer community indefinitely. Cohort 1 alumni have already committed to leading the post-funding Tech Circle as volunteers.
```
