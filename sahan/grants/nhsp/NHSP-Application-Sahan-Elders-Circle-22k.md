## 2. Project Title
**Sahan Elders Circle: Belonging, Mentorship and Safety**

## 3. Project Description

Sahan Elders Circle is a 12-month, community-based project that brings together Black and newcomer seniors (55+) in Northeast Calgary — with strong representation from Somali, Sudanese, Eritrean, Ethiopian, and Oromo communities — for regular social connection, peer mentoring, and elder-abuse and fraud-awareness education.

Many seniors in this community arrived in Canada later in life, face language barriers, and are at risk of isolation once outside their immediate family. The project addresses this directly through a recurring **Elders Circle** (a bi-weekly drop-in gathering for storytelling, tea, and peer connection), a structured **Peer Mentorship Program** pairing established seniors with newly arrived seniors, **intergenerational mentoring days** connecting seniors with youth, and **elder-abuse and financial-fraud awareness workshops** delivered in Somali and other community languages.

Unlike a program built around a single big investment (like equipment or renovations), this project is deliberately low-cost and recurring — it prioritizes consistent, frequent contact over the 12 months, which is what actually reduces isolation and builds trust.

## 5. How Seniors Lead and Shape the Project

- **4 Senior Peer Mentors**, recruited from the community, help design the Elders Circle format, identify workshop topics, and lead peer mentoring relationships.
- Seniors choose the storytelling and cultural themes for each Circle session.
- Feedback from participants after each workshop and event shapes the next quarter's programming.

## 11. Use of Funds (Narrative)

Funds will support a recurring, low-overhead program of social connection, peer mentorship, and safety education for Black and newcomer seniors in Northeast Calgary. The largest cost is the part-time coordinator who recruits and trains peer mentors, schedules the bi-weekly Elders Circle, and liaises with community-language interpreters and workshop partners. The remaining funds directly support participants: refreshments and venue costs for regular gatherings, honoraria for peer mentors and workshop facilitators, culturally appropriate printed materials, and community meals that specifically reach isolated seniors. No funds are allocated to capital purchases — the project is designed to run on modest, recurring costs rather than one-time investments.
