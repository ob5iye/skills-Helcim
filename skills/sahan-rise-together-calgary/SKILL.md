# Rise Together: Pathways for Newcomer Women — Sahan Heritage Foundation (Calgary)

A working reference for Sahan's application to Alberta's **Community Initiatives Program (CIP) Project Grant**. The program is a culturally grounded employment-readiness, English-language, and community-belonging program for newcomer women in Calgary — primarily East African and Black communities, many of them single mothers facing language, employment, and childcare barriers.

---

## 1. Snapshot

- **Applicant:** Sahan Heritage Foundation (Calgary). Sister society to Horn Youth Services Foundation (operating as KULAN), Edmonton, which designed and delivered the Edmonton version.
- **Program model (lean plan):** 1 cohort · 15 women · 12 weekly Saturdays · 60 contact hours.
- **CIP ask:** $24,000
- **Sahan cash match:** $11,000 ($6,000 operating reserve retained out of $17,000 on hand)
- **In-kind match:** $15,960
- **Total matching:** $26,960 (112% of the ask)
- **Total project cost:** $50,960 (CIP share 47.1%, under the 50% cap)
- **Venue:** Donated hall — Somali Canadian Society of Calgary, 3940 29 Street NE, Calgary, AB T1Y 6B6 (in-kind, with letter)

---

## 2. CIP essentials

- Funder: Government of Alberta, Arts, Culture and Status of Women.
- Maximum ask $75,000; deadlines **February 15** and **August 15**; apply via **GATE Front Office** (register at least 10 business days before the deadline); report within 18 months of grant payment.
- **Matching:** cash and/or in-kind contributions must be **equal to or greater than** the request. New staff **wages** must be matched with **cash** (this program uses contractors, so it avoids that rule). Total Government of Alberta funding **cannot exceed 50%** of total project cost.
- Standardized in-kind rates: unskilled volunteer labour $20/hr; skilled labour $40/hr.

---

## 3. Program design

- 15 women per cohort; one Saturday per week for 12 weeks; 5 contact hours per Saturday = 60 hours.
- Weekly schedule: 9:45–10:00 arrival & sign-in; 10:00 am–1:00 pm Session 1 (Job Readiness); 1:00–2:00 pm hot lunch (women + children); 2:00–4:00 pm Session 2 (English + mentorship); 4:00–4:15 pm pickup & debrief.

---

## 4. Curriculum modules (the job, and why)

**Morning — Job Readiness (2 sessions each):**

1. **Self-assessment & transferable skills** — surfaces skills she already has; builds confidence to start.
2. **Creating a resume** — a Canadian-style, ESL-adapted resume; the core document needed to apply for any job.
3. **Cover letters & online applications** — how jobs are actually applied for here (job boards, online forms).
4. **Interview preparation & mock interviews** — practice and feedback turn applications into offers.
5. **Workplace communication** — phone, professional email, and workplace norms.
6. **Networking & next-steps plan** — builds contacts, matches a mentor, sets her action plan.

**Afternoon (every week):** conversational/workplace English tied to the morning topic; small-group mentor circles.

**Added supports:** 1:1 employment coaching; digital-literacy practice on 6 shared laptops; the Bridges cross-community event; graduation in Week 12.

---

## 5. Group sizes (how many in each session)

- Job-readiness workshop and English: all 15 women together.
- Mentor circles: 3–4 women per mentor.
- 1:1 employment coaching: individual.
- Childcare room: 15–25 children with 3 registered babysitters.

---

## 6. Childcare, food, staffing

- **Childcare:** separate room, full program day, 3 registered/certified babysitters, sign-in/out logs, halal food.
- **Food:** morning snack; hot halal lunch (women + children together); afternoon snack; vegetarian option weekly.
- **Staff (all contract):** lead facilitator, employment coach, ESL instructor (TESL-certified), program coordinator; 3 babysitters; 1–2 volunteers; 3–4 rotating mentors.

---

## 7. Venue

- Somali Canadian Society of Calgary hall, 3940 29 Street NE, Calgary, AB T1Y 6B6.
- Donated free of charge as an **in-kind** contribution (~$9,000 fair market value for the program period).
- The Society's letter, on its letterhead, doubles as the **venue titleholder's letter of support** required by CIP.
- Action: confirm with Community Grants that donated facility space is valued at **fair market value** (not the $20/hr labour rate). Keep dollar value out of the signed letter; document the FMV separately for the budget.

## 8. Bridges cross-community event

- Around Week 8, the standard afternoon session is replaced with a joint cooking, storytelling, and skill-share with 5–8 women from a partner organization in another Calgary cultural or faith community (**partner name to confirm**).
- Builds tolerance, anti-racism, and social inclusion; gives participants exposure to working women from another community; a strong differentiator for funders.

## 10. Scaling to 26 women

- 26 women = two cohorts of ~13. This is essentially two programs (24 session-days; double staff, childcare, catering) and needs roughly double the funding — raise the CIP ask to about **$33,000–$38,000** (cash and in-kind stay similar), still under the $75,000 cap.
- At a **$24,000** ask, the lean, fully-resourced optimum is **one cohort of ~15**. Best path for 26: run 15 now and waitlist 11 for the next intake.

## 13. Why it fits CIP

- New initiative in Calgary (not status-quo operations).
- Meaningful cash co-investment (~41% of the match, plus a signed in-kind venue letter) signals organizational commitment while preserving a $6,000 operating reserve.
- Gender equity; newcomer integration; employment outcomes (resumes, interviews, workplace English, digital literacy).
- Social inclusion / anti-racism via the Bridges component.
- Serves multiple equity-deserving groups: newcomers, women, racialized and low-income people, and children.
- Addresses a documented barrier — childcare is the reason many single mothers miss existing programs.
- Registered/certified childcare signals professional duty of care.
