# CIP Newcomer Women Program — Application Skill

A reference skill for applying to **Alberta's Community Initiatives Program (CIP) Project Grant** with a strong, fundable program serving newcomer women — especially single mothers facing language, employment, and childcare barriers.

---

## 1. CIP Program Essentials

- **Funder:** Government of Alberta, Arts, Culture and Status of Women
- **Maximum ask:** $75,000
- **Annual deadlines:** February 15 and August 15
- **Notification:** ~6 months after deadline
- **Application portal:** GATE Front Office (register at least 10 business days before deadline)
- **Reporting:** Projects must be completed and fully reported within 18 months of grant payment

### Eligible Organizations

Non-profits registered under one of:
- Alberta's Societies Act, Companies Act (Part 9), Business Corporations Act (Part 21), Agriculture Societies Act, Libraries Act, Métis Settlements Act
- Federal Income Tax Act (charity status), Indian Act, or a Special Act
- Must have operated for at least one full year, have an Alberta address actively monitored, and (if a charity) operate in Alberta

### Ineligible Projects (Do NOT Apply For)

- Construction, renovation, retrofitting, or land purchases
- Religious observance activities or church sanctuaries
- Gaming-related costs
- K-12 schools, post-secondary institutions, municipalities, for-profits
- Programs that started before the application date
- Events that already happened
- Ongoing programs (CIP funds new initiatives or enhancements, not status-quo operations)
- National/international sporting events eligible under Tourism and Sport's Major Sport Event Funding Program

### Eligible Project Types

- New community programs or enhancements to existing programs
- Community events and experiences (require letter of support from venue titleholder)
- Technology (audio/visual, computer hardware, licensing, website/app development)
- Portable equipment (vehicles, uniforms, furnishings, portable office equipment)

---

## 2. Matching Requirements (Critical)

CIP requires applicants to **match the requested amount with cash and/or in-kind contributions equal to or exceeding the request**. If you ask $X, your matching must be at least $X.

### Cash vs. In-Kind Rules

- **New staff wages require cash matching.** In-kind cannot cover wage portions.
- **Equipment purchases or new/expanded programs may require higher cash matching.**
- Total Government of Alberta funding (CIP + any other GoA source) **cannot exceed 50% of total project cost**.

### Standardized In-Kind Rates

| Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| Unskilled volunteer labour | $20/hour |
| Skilled labour (artists, professionals, technical) | $40/hour |
| Heavy equipment with operator | $120/hour |

### Ineligible Matching Sources

- Other Government of Alberta funding
- Cash toward ineligible expenses
- Regular operations wages
- Volunteer time on planning meetings, fundraising, or grant writing
- Donated materials already owned by the organization
- Personal donations of equipment, materials, or services

---

## 3. Required Application Documents

- Board member list with positions and contact info
- Most recent financial statements (audited or unaudited), signed by 2 signing authorities
- Bank statements for ALL accounts, each signed by 2 signing authorities
- Application for Electronic Payment (AEP) + void cheque
- Estimates / supplier quotes for each project activity
- Donated in-kind documentation (letters on company letterhead) if claiming in-kind match
- Third-party agreement if applicable
- Letter of support from venue titleholder (community events only)
- Job description for any new staff position requested
- **Optional but powerful:** letters of support from partners, community members, government, businesses

## 5. Proven Program Template — "Rise Together: Pathways for Newcomer Women"

A culturally grounded employment readiness, English language, and community-belonging program for newcomer women — many of whom are single mothers facing language, employment, and childcare barriers.

### Target Population

- Newcomer women, primarily East African and Black communities
- Women who do not speak English fluently
- Single mothers who are unemployed or under-employed
- Women who have lived in Canada for years but never entered the Canadian workforce

### Program Length & Cohort Size

- **15 women per cohort**
- **12 weeks per cohort**
- One Saturday per week = **12 program days**
- Total reach: 15 women + 15–25 children + 4 mentors + 5–8 cross-community partner attendees

### Weekly Saturday Schedule

| Time | Activity | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 9:45 – 10:00 am | Arrival, sign-in, children handed to babysitters | 15 min |
| 10:00 am – 1:00 pm | Session 1 — Job Readiness Workshop | 3 hrs |
| 1:00 – 2:00 pm | Hot lunch (women + children together) | 1 hr |
| 2:00 – 4:00 pm | Session 2 — Conversational English + Mentor Time | 2 hrs |
| 4:00 – 4:15 pm | Pickup, weekly handout, debrief | 15 min |

**Contact hours per participant:** 60 hours total per cohort

### Session 1 Content (Job Readiness)

- Weeks 1–2: Self-assessment, transferable skills, Canadian workplace intro
- Weeks 3–4: Resume building (ESL-adapted templates)
- Weeks 5–6: Cover letters, online job applications
- Weeks 7–8: Interview preparation, mock interviews
- Weeks 9–10: Workplace communication (phone, email, norms)
- Weeks 11–12: Networking, mentor matching, next-steps planning

### Session 2 Content (English + Mentorship)

- First hour: Workplace-vocabulary ESL tied to that week's Session 1 topic
- Second hour: Small-group mentor circles (3–4 women per mentor)

### Children's Room

- Separate room at the venue, available 9:45 am – 4:15 pm
- **2 registered/certified babysitters** on-site the full day
- Capacity: up to 20 children, mixed ages infant to ~10
- Setup: play mats, age-grouped toy stations, nap area, snack/lunch table
- Sign-in/out logs every Saturday (safety + insurance)
- Children eat lunch with mothers at 1pm

### Food (Every Saturday)

| Time | Meal |
|---|---|
| 10:00 am | Morning snack (fruit, pastries, tea, coffee) |
| 1:00 – 2:00 pm | Hot lunch (rotating culturally familiar menu) for women + children |
| 2:30 pm | Afternoon snack |

All food halal-only, with vegetarian option weekly.

### Staffing Per Saturday

| Role | Number |
|---|---|
| Lead Facilitator (Job Readiness) | 1 |
| ESL Instructor (TESL-certified) | 1 |
| Registered Babysitters | 2 |
| Program Coordinator | 1 |
| Volunteer Support | 1–2 |
| Mentors (rotating) | 3–4 |

### Cross-Community Bridges Event

**One session per cohort (around Week 8)** replaces standard Session 2 with a joint cooking + storytelling + skill-share with a small delegation (5–8 women) from a partner community organization. Builds tolerance, social inclusion, and anti-racism outcomes. Same venue, same childcare. Powerful differentiator on the application.

### Materials Each Participant Receives

- ESL-adapted workbook
- Resume and cover letter templates (print + digital)
- Bus pass for 12 weeks
- Mentor introduction packet (Week 6)
- Certificate of completion (Week 12)

### Outcomes to Measure

- Number completing the 12-week cohort (target 13/15)
- Number with finalized resume (target 15/15)
- Number completing mock interview (target 13/15)
- Pre/post confidence in workplace English
- Number applying to at least one job by program end (target 10/15)
- Number of mentor relationships established (target 15)
- Number of children safely cared for (target 15–25)
- Attendance rate per participant (target ≥75%)

### Matching Strategy

For any CIP ask of $X, document at least $X in matching. Aim for a cushion of 10–25% above minimum.

**Easy in-kind sources to document:**

| Source | Calculation Approach |
|---|---|
| Mentor hours (skilled) | mentors × hours × $40/hr |
| Donated program space | hours × $20/hr |
| Volunteer admin/intake support | hours × $20/hr |
| Partner facilitators (skilled) | people × hours × $40/hr |
| ESL volunteer tutors (skilled) | hours × $40/hr |

**If asking for new staff wages, secure that portion in cash before applying.** Sources: complementary smaller grants, partner cash contributions, board fundraising commitments.

## 8. Why This Program Wins Funding

It hits every CIP-favored marker simultaneously:

- **New initiative** (not ongoing) ✓
- **Gender equity** (women-only, addresses domestic labour + isolation barriers) ✓
- **Newcomer integration** ✓
- **Employment outcomes** (resumes, interviews, workplace English) ✓
- **Social inclusion / anti-racism** (Bridges component) ✓
- **Multiple equity-deserving groups served** (newcomers, women, racialized, low-income, children) ✓
- **Addresses a real, documented barrier** (childcare is the reason single moms miss existing programs) ✓
- **Demonstrated community demand** (existing CV writing work + drop-in feedback) ✓
- **Strong partnerships** (cross-community letters of support) ✓
- **Registered/certified childcare** signals professional duty of care, not informal minding ✓

The cross-community partnership component (e.g., joint sessions with women from another faith or cultural community) is the strongest differentiator — most newcomer-women applications don't have it.
